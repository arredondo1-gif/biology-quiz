Quick review

The minimum you need to know for each topic.

1Scientific investigation and experiments

TermKey idea
ScienceA systematic way of studying the natural world using observations, evidence, experiments and logical reasoning.
ObservationInformation collected directly with the senses or instruments. "The plant has six yellow leaves."
InferenceA possible explanation based on observations and prior knowledge. "The plant may lack nutrients."
Scientific questionTestable and answerable with evidence. Opinion questions ("Which is prettier?") are not scientific.
HypothesisA specific, testable prediction based on science: "If ..., then ... because ..."
Independent variableThe one factor the scientist changes.
Dependent variableWhat is measured or observed.
Controlled variablesKept the same so the test is fair.
Control groupDoes not get the treatment; it is the standard for comparison.
RepetitionShows whether results are consistent and reliable.
CommunicationLets others evaluate, repeat and build on the work.
Trick: the independent variable is what I change; the dependent variable depends on it and is what I measure.

3Common parts of cells

All cells, prokaryotic and eukaryotic, share these four parts.

PartJob
Plasma membraneBoundary of the cell. Selectively permeable: controls what enters and leaves, removes wastes, helps keep homeostasis.
CytoplasmJellylike material inside the cell where many chemical reactions happen.
RibosomesMake proteins.
DNAGenetic material with the instructions for cell activities.
Proteins are essential for cell structure, growth and many cell functions. DNA gives the instructions; ribosomes build the proteins.

4Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells

ProkaryoticEukaryotic
SizeSmaller, simplerLarger, more complex
NucleusNoYes, surrounded by a membrane
DNAIn a region of the cytoplasmMainly in the nucleus
OrganellesNo membrane-bound organellesMembrane-bound organelles
Both haveRibosomes, cytoplasm, plasma membrane, DNA
ExamplesBacteria, archaeaAnimals, plants, fungi, protists
"Pro" = before, "eu" = true. Prokaryotes have no true nucleus.

5Ecosystems

TermKey idea
EcosystemAll the living and nonliving things in an area, plus their interactions (forest, desert, lake, pond, grassland).
BioticLiving: trees, fish, bacteria, fungi, insects.
AbioticNonliving: sunlight, water, temperature, rocks, minerals, oxygen.
HabitatThe place where an organism normally lives.
NicheThe organism's role: how it gets resources and interacts with others.
Frog: habitat = freshwater pond; niche = eats insects and is prey for larger animals. Habitat is the address; niche is the job. Changes in abiotic factors (like a drought) affect living things: some migrate, struggle or die.
